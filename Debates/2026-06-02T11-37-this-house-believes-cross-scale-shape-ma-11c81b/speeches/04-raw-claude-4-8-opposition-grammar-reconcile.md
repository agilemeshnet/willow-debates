OPPOSITION reconciles against PROPOSITION's definitions.

DEFINITION 1 — SHAPE
> "A shape is a pattern of relations between elements, specifiable without reference to what the elements are made of. Two systems share a shape when their elements can be placed in correspondence such that the relations in one map onto the relations in the other. A shape is not the elements, not the substrate — it is the constraint structure."

DISPUTE (minimal). The first and third sentences: ACCEPT in full. The second sentence carries the whole disagreement of this debate inside one modal verb: "can be placed in correspondence." Almost any two systems CAN be placed in SOME correspondence; unconstrained existential quantification over mappings is precisely the door through which apophenia walks. MINIMAL FIX: amend the second sentence to "Two systems share a shape when their elements can be placed in correspondence UNDER THE NAMED ADMISSIBLE MAPPING such that the relations in one map onto the relations in the other." This simply imports the constraint the Proposition already accepted in Definition 1 of the Opposition. With that four-word insertion: ACCEPT.

DEFINITION 2 — STRUCTURAL INVARIANT
> "A structural invariant is a property or relation that is preserved under a specified class of transformation. There is no invariance 'in general' — only invariance with respect to a named mapping. To claim a structural invariant is to claim both the property and the transformation under which it holds."

ACCEPT. Identical in substance to the Opposition's own. No amendment.

DEFINITION 3 — SCALE
> "A scale is a level of description at which a system's elements and their relations are enumerated. To cross scales is to map between descriptions at different levels of granularity or organisational hierarchy, where the elements at one level may be composites, abstractions, or emergents of elements at another. Two scales are distinct when neither can be trivially read off the other."

ACCEPT. The distinctness condition ("neither can be trivially read off the other") is the symmetric form of our own and is acceptable.
