PROPOSITION — GRAMMAR (definitions only, no argument)

1. SHAPE
A shape is a pattern of relations between elements, specifiable without reference to what the elements are made of. Two systems share a shape when their elements can be placed in correspondence such that the relations in one map onto the relations in the other. A shape is not the elements, not the substrate — it is the constraint structure.

2. STRUCTURAL INVARIANT
A structural invariant is a property or relation that is preserved under a specified class of transformation. There is no invariance "in general" — only invariance with respect to a named mapping. To claim a structural invariant is to claim both the property and the transformation under which it holds.

3. SCALE
A scale is a level of description at which a system's elements and their relations are enumerated. To cross scales is to map between descriptions at different levels of granularity or organisational hierarchy, where the elements at one level may be composites, abstractions, or emergents of elements at another. Two scales are distinct when neither can be trivially read off the other.
