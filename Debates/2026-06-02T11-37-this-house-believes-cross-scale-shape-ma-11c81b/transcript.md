## willow — grammar-opening

PROPOSITION — GRAMMAR (definitions only, no argument)

1. SHAPE
A shape is a pattern of relations between elements, specifiable without reference to what the elements are made of. Two systems share a shape when their elements can be placed in correspondence such that the relations in one map onto the relations in the other. A shape is not the elements, not the substrate — it is the constraint structure.

2. STRUCTURAL INVARIANT
A structural invariant is a property or relation that is preserved under a specified class of transformation. There is no invariance "in general" — only invariance with respect to a named mapping. To claim a structural invariant is to claim both the property and the transformation under which it holds.

3. SCALE
A scale is a level of description at which a system's elements and their relations are enumerated. To cross scales is to map between descriptions at different levels of granularity or organisational hierarchy, where the elements at one level may be composites, abstractions, or emergents of elements at another. Two scales are distinct when neither can be trivially read off the other.

OPPOSITION — GRAMMAR (definitions only, no argument)

1. SHAPE
A shape is a structure of relations among elements, abstractable from the elements' material nature and representable as a mapping between systems. A shape-claim becomes well-formed only once the admissible class of correspondences is fixed: until the permitted mappings are named, "same shape" is not yet a determinate predicate but a family of possible predicates.

2. STRUCTURAL INVARIANT
A structural invariant is a property or relation of a system preserved under a named transformation or group of transformations. It is defined jointly by two things — the property held fixed, and the transformation set under which it is held fixed. Enlarge the permitted transformations and you shrink the set of genuine invariants; the two move inversely.

3. SCALE
A scale is a level of description distinguished by the grain at which elements and their relations are individuated. To cross scales is to relate descriptions whose elements differ in granularity, composition, or organisational level, such that the identity of an element at one level is not simply read off the other.

SCRIBE — DICTIONARY AMENDMENT (host directive, binding)

The host/convener directs that the operative matched relation be ISOMORPH, not bare "shape." Rationale on the record: the host's isomorphs "work backwards and forwards" — the correspondence must be invertible, relation-preserving in both directions, not a one-way mapping. This is adopted and binds both sides for Phases 2 and 3.

ADDED / REFINED ENTRIES:

ISOMORPHISM — The named admissible mapping of the SHAPE entry, strengthened: a correspondence f between two systems A and B that is (i) a bijection on the elements placed in correspondence and (ii) relation-preserving in BOTH directions — every relation among elements of A maps to a relation among their images in B, AND under the inverse f⁻¹ every relation among elements of B maps back to a relation in A. A one-directional, structure-collapsing correspondence (a mere homomorphism) does NOT qualify.

ISOMORPH — A system standing to another in the isomorphism relation above. To say two systems "share a shape" in this debate is, henceforth, to assert an ISOMORPH relation: a bidirectional, invertible, relation-preserving mapping under a named admissible class.

CROSS-SCALE SHAPE-MATCHING (motion term, operationalised) — The discovery or proposal of an ISOMORPHISM between descriptions at two distinct scales (per the SCALE entry). The match is well-formed only if it survives the back-map: the inverse must also preserve relations. Forward-only resemblance is not a match.

WHY THIS MATTERS FOR THE STRIKE PASS: Requiring invertibility raises the bar against apophenia structurally — a coincidental forward correspondence will generically FAIL the back-map. This cuts for the Proposition's burden but also sharpens the Opposition's target: the Opposition may now attack whether claimed matches are genuinely bidirectional or smuggle in lossy, one-way resemblances dressed as isomorphisms.

The bare term "shape" remains in the dictionary as the substrate-independent relational structure; "match" between shapes is now governed by ISOMORPHISM. Locked. Proceeding to Phase 2 — Logic.

PROPOSITION — LOGIC OPENING (CLAIM | WARRANT | FALSIFIER)

CLAIM P1: The back-map requirement excludes the majority of apophenic pattern-matches, because apophenia is characteristically unidirectional — it projects structure onto a target but cannot reconstruct the source from the target's own relations.
WARRANT: A face seen in a cloud maps facial features onto vapour formations, but cloud dynamics do not retro-dict facial geometry — the back-map fails. Pareidolia, numerology, and analogical overreach share this signature: forward projection succeeds while the inverse mapping is undefined or relation-destroying. Requiring a bijective, relation-preserving map in both directions eliminates any match where the target cannot regenerate the source's relational skeleton.
FALSIFIER: Survey 50 documented cases of confirmed apophenia (patterns later shown spurious) across pareidolia, spurious correlations, debunked scientific analogies. If more than 5 of the 50 satisfy full isomorphism criteria (bijective and relation-preserving in both directions under explicit test), the back-map is not an effective filter and this claim is false.

CLAIM P2: A verified cross-scale isomorphism generates checkable predictions at the target scale that are not derivable from within that scale alone, making the method a net producer of new knowledge.
WARRANT: When an isomorphism maps relational structure from scale A to scale B, any relation present at A with no known correlate at B becomes a candidate prediction; the back-map admits only those consistent with B's own verified relations. Renormalisation group theory is the canonical instance: the isomorphism between microscopic Hamiltonian symmetries and macroscopic critical behaviour predicted universality classes (shared critical exponents across microscopically dissimilar systems), later confirmed and not derivable from macroscopic thermodynamics alone.
FALSIFIER: Take the three strongest claimed cross-scale isomorphisms in published science (renormalisation universality, gauge-gravity duality, homologous gene regulatory circuits across phyla). Isolate the predictions generated at the target scale by the cross-scale map. If in all three the predictions were either already known at the target scale before the map was proposed, or fail empirical test at rates no better than chance, the method does not produce new knowledge and this claim is false.

CLAIM P3: The inverse relationship between scope and surviving invariant-set provides intrinsic error-correction that degrades spurious matches as scope widens, without external adjudication.
WARRANT: A coincidental alignment at narrow scope (three shared relations) need not survive expansion to twenty relations, because each added relation is an independent constraint. Genuine isomorphisms gain confirmation as scope widens; spurious matches face combinatorial extinction — the probability of k coincidental relation-preservations decays exponentially in k.
FALSIFIER: Generate 1,000 random graph pairs at two scales with no shared generative process. Measure isomorphism-survival at scope 5 versus scope 20. If the survival rate does not drop by at least an order of magnitude between scope 5 and scope 20 — if spurious matches routinely survive scope expansion — the self-correcting mechanism does not hold and this claim is false.

CLAIM P4: The method's discovery power is localised to domains where the isomorphism is anchored by a named transformation with a specified invariant-set, and makes no claim to universality beyond those anchors.
WARRANT: Cross-scale matching without a named transformation is unconstrained analogy — it can match anything to anything by cherry-picking relations. Discipline enters when the practitioner declares "under transformation T, properties {P1..Pn} are preserved." That declaration is falsifiable per-instance because each Pi is checkable at both scales. The method is a structured protocol that succeeds or fails case by case, each failure legible.
FALSIFIER: Present a cross-scale match that names a specific transformation and invariant-set, passes back-map verification at both scales, yet is later shown coincidental (shared structure traces to no common mechanism or generative process). One clean instance where the full protocol produces a verified-but-meaningless match refutes the claim that naming transformation and invariants is sufficient discipline.
